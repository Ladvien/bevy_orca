## Iteration 2 — 2026-10-30 — every input gets a documented finite answer

**What changed.** `new_velocity_in` promised "the result is always finite" but guarded only `max_speed`, `pref_vel`, `dt` and `time_horizon`. A hostile sweep (200 000 single solves with one hostile field each plus all-field chaos; 20 000 crowd steps of 0–11 members) found **6 505** and **1 709** non-finite velocities: an agent's own non-finite velocity or radius, a neighbour's non-finite or negative-radius state, non-finite or scaled walls, a `max_speed` above ~1.8e19, subnormal `dt`/`time_horizon`, and finite values whose arithmetic overflows `f32`. Now each has one documented answer: own state unusable → `Vec2::ZERO`; wall not a unit normal (`Vec2::is_normalized`) or approach not finite → `Vec2::ZERO` (walls are hard); neighbour unusable, or whose line overflows → skipped; `max_speed` whose square overflows → `ZERO`; `dt`/`time_horizon` whose reciprocal overflows → the clamped wish; `linear_program1` treats a NaN/infinite discriminant as infeasible (RVO2's branch for every finite one). `Crowd` keeps members with unusable state out of the neighbour grid, so they take no `max_neighbours` place. Sweep after: **0** and **0**, no panics. Every sane solve is bit-identical. Assumed decision (owner may override): an unusable own state returns `Vec2::ZERO` rather than `new_velocity_in` returning `Option<Vec2>` (breaking) or the clamped wish (walks through neighbours) — conservative, non-breaking, the answer a bad `max_speed` already got, and self-healing once the zero is written back.

## Iteration 3 — 2026-10-30 — a packed crowd allocates nothing and costs half as much (#7)

**What changed.** `Crowd::solve` ordered each member's candidate list with `sort_by`, the stable driftsort, which takes a heap scratch once the list outgrows its 4 KiB stack buffer (170 candidates of 24 bytes), and then used only the first `max_neighbours`. Every member of a packed crowd allocated once per tick and paid for ordering ~200 candidates to use 10. Now `select_nth_unstable_by` moves the nearest `max_neighbours` to the front, `truncate` drops the rest and `sort_unstable_by` orders the kept ones — both in place. The order `(distance by total_cmp, key, index)` is strict because an index appears once per list, so every neighbour list and every velocity is bit-identical. The bench gained a fifth scene, `rally` (units hex-packed 6 cm apart around one point, 5 m range, 89–205 candidates per member on average), because none of the four could reach 170 candidates (densest 65 to 92). The bench's caller-side parallel ring selects the same way.
