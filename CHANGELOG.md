# Changelog

All notable changes to this crate are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the crate follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Static obstacles: `Obstacles`, `ObstaclesBuilder`, `Crowd::solve_around` and `CrowdParams::obstacle_horizon`.** The geometry that does not change — buildings, a wall with a doorway, the edge of the map — is built once (`add_box`, `add_solid` and `add_enclosure` in either winding, `add_wall`; a NaN, a zero-length edge, a polygon with no area or one that crosses itself is an `ObstacleError` at `build`) and handed to `Crowd::solve_around`, which does what `solve` does and also casts RVO2's obstacle lines on every member: for each edge within `obstacle_horizon · max_speed + radius`, the line tangent to its velocity obstacle at the point nearest the member's current velocity (a face, a cutoff circle round a vertex, or a leg that lets a walker bend round a convex corner without braking for it). They are hard walls in the form `new_velocity_in` already took (`(unit normal, c)` with `v · normal <= c`), so the solver is unchanged: ahead of every neighbour line, never relaxed, and the member takes all of the avoidance. `Obstacles::push_walls` appends them for a caller of the single-agent API. An empty set answers exactly what `solve` does. Before this a host built the half-planes itself, or did without: Sanctuary's `crowd.rs` passes no walls and pushes its villagers out of the boxes after the step; bevy_emerge's crowd preview casts four axis-aligned half-planes from the 4-neighbour solid cells.
- **Against RVO2, built as shipped:** on 60 000 random convex polygons, walls and concave polygons (RVO2 as a flat list, because its kd-tree splits an obstacle at another's line and so its lines depend on the partition) the same lines and a solved velocity within `3e-6`. In one place it is better on purpose: RVO2 skips a "foreign" leg because the neighbouring edge casts that line, but a wall with no thickness has only its own other side for a neighbour, which a walker on this side cannot see, so a walker a few ulps from the wall's face, moving along it, gets no line and walks in: 5 874 of 400 000 random walkers touching a random shape, by up to a whole step (4.3 cm at 1.3 m/s, 30 Hz); none here.
- `examples/street.rs`: four buildings and three streams of walkers crossing and turning their corners, the obstacle API end to end, printing the closest any disc came to a wall (never below zero).
- `examples/bench.rs`: three scenes, `bottleneck_obst` (the bottleneck's wall as `Obstacles`), `city` (walkers on Manhattan routes along the streets of a grid of buildings, as `Obstacles`) and `city_pushout` (the same city the way a host does it without: nothing in the solve and a push-out of any walker whose disc ends a step inside a building, with `pushed` and `push_max` printed), in the timing and the `arrive` sections; and `ORCA_OBST_HORIZON=s`.
- `examples/bench.rs`: a fifth scene, `rally` — units hex-packed around one point with a 5 m neighbour range, about 200 candidates per member at 10 000 agents — so the bench measures a packed crowd, where choosing the neighbours costs more than solving for them.
- `examples/bench.rs`: the `arrive` section now prints `t50_s` and `t90_s` (when half and nine tenths had arrived), runs the bottleneck at 100, 200 and 400 agents and the 20-agent circle on the `circle_swap` example's wider ring, and takes `ORCA_SEED=n` (another crowd of the same scene, for a spread) and `ORCA_SERIAL=1` (time only the serial forward run); `ORCA_SCENES` now restricts both sections.
- `examples/bench.rs`: `speed_max`, the largest solved speed on any step as a fraction of its member's `max_speed`, on every measured line and every `arrive` line (`1.000000` is the bound; above `1.000001` the answer left the speed disc).

### Fixed

- `new_velocity_in` (and so `new_velocity` and `Crowd::solve`) returned NaN or infinite velocities, despite "the result is always finite", for input its guards did not look at: an agent's own non-finite velocity or radius, a neighbour's non-finite or negative-radius state, a non-finite or non-unit wall, a `max_speed` above about `1.8e19`, a subnormal `dt` or `time_horizon`, and finite values whose arithmetic overflows `f32` (agents near `±f32::MAX`). A hostile sweep found 6 505 non-finite answers in 200 000 single solves and 1 709 in 20 000 crowd steps; it now finds none. Each case has one documented answer: an agent with unusable own state, or an unusable wall, holds still (`Vec2::ZERO`); an unusable neighbour, or one whose constraint overflows, is skipped; a `max_speed` whose square overflows gives `Vec2::ZERO`; a `dt` or `time_horizon` whose reciprocal overflows means no time passes. Finite, in-range input is bit-identical to before.
- `Crowd::solve` allocated once per member per tick whenever more than 170 candidates were in range, despite "`Crowd` allocates nothing after its buffers are warm" (#7): the candidate list was ordered with the standard library's stable sort, which takes a heap scratch above 4 KiB. A packed crowd — a rally point, a spawn cluster, a queue — reaches that with an ordinary range: 260 619 allocations in 480 warm steps of the bench's 1 000-member rally, 4 050 400 at 10 000. A candidate list longer than 16 and than twice `max_neighbours` is now narrowed to its nearest `max_neighbours` by an in-place selection and the rest sorted in place; a shorter one is sorted whole and cut. The order is the same strict `(distance, key, index)`, so every neighbour list and every solved velocity is bit-identical (every bench hash equal), a warm solve allocates nothing, and the step is cheaper where candidates outnumber neighbours: the 10 000-member rally 103 ms → 49 ms, the bottleneck 20–39% and the open field 3–18% (median of three paired runs); the corridor and the circle swap, which mostly stay under 16 candidates, do not move.
- `Crowd::solve` offered every member with a `pref_vel` to its neighbours as one that steps aside (`avoids: true`), including two that do not: a member whose wish is not finite (skipped, `None`) and a member whose `max_speed` is not finite, not positive or so large its square overflows (solved to `Vec2::ZERO`). A mover took half of the avoidance against a member that held still, which is the drift-through that `avoids: false` exists to stop (#8). One test, `steers`, now decides both the solver's skip and the flag, and the `max_speed` guard is the one `new_velocity_in` already had, lifted into `usable_speed`, so they cannot drift apart. A member with a finite wish and a usable speed is offered as before, so every other solve is bit-identical. `README` said a non-finite wish returns `Vec2::ZERO`; it returns `None`, as the rustdoc always said.
- `new_velocity_in` (and so `new_velocity` and `Crowd::solve`) answered `Vec2::ZERO`, not the clamped wish, for a finite `pref_vel` longer than about `1.8e19` (#9): the speed-disc clamp compared `|pref|²` with `max_speed²`, the square overflowed to `+inf`, and normalising by an infinite length gave zero, so an agent whose navigator said "go, as fast as you can" (`offset / time_left` with a tiny `time_left`) stopped dead — `(1e20, 0)` at `max_speed = 1.5` gave `(0, 0)` where `(1.5, 0)` is the answer, with no neighbour, with a wall, and at `dt = 0` alike. A wish whose square overflows is now scaled by the exact power of two `2^-96` before it is normalised, as LAPACK's `dnrm2` scales large values; one whose square is finite takes the code it always did, so no other solve moves by a bit (402 299 random solves hash equal to before, and every bench hash). RVO2 and Detour's `dtVlen` have the same unguarded square. A probe over 197 701 huge wishes gives 6 zero answers where it gave 147 998, and the 6 left have own velocities of 1e9 and above.
- `new_velocity_in` (and so `new_velocity` and `Crowd::solve`) answered faster than `max_speed`, despite "result magnitude is clamped to `max_speed`": on random packs of 2 to 40 members, 15 616 of 1 896 840 `Crowd::solve` velocities (0.8%) were over `1.000001×` their member's limit and 701 over `1.001×`, the worst 77×; among 100 000 ordinary single solves 2 893 were over and the worst was 6.3× (a two-neighbour step with no wall gave 1.86×), with two neighbours standing on the agent 5 122 and 13×, and with a fast own velocity (`1e10` and up) up to `1e8×`. The reference implementation does the same (RVO2: 14 805 of the same number of velocities, worst 64×). `linear_program3` built each projected line through the intersection of two nearly parallel lines, up to `1/EPSILON` from the origin, and `linear_program1` then subtracted two huge, nearly equal squares to find where the line meets the speed disc and added two huge, nearly opposite vectors to answer. Every half-plane is now stored at its point nearest the origin (`point = det(direction, point) · perp(direction)`), and `linear_program3` builds each projected line from the two lines' distances to the origin, `det(d_j − d_i, v) = c_j − c_i`, instead of through their intersection: the same half-planes and the same three stages, with every number the disc test and the answer use no larger than the disc. The same sweeps now find no velocity over `1.000001×` (0 of 1 896 840 crowd solves, 0 of 400 000 single solves, 0 of 200 000 hostile solves), and on 40 000 random cases the answer is feasible, no farther from the wish than the best of a dense sample of the feasible region, and as good a minimax as the sample in the over-constrained ones, as before. `NOTICE` credits the change from RVO2.
- `new_velocity_in` (and so `Crowd::solve`) let a wish pass a `max_speed` whose square underflows unclamped (a wish `1e-38` long through a limit of `1e-40`: 117× the limit), because the disc test compared with a square that rounded to zero. Such a `max_speed` (below about `1.1e-19`) is now refused with `Vec2::ZERO`, like one whose square overflows.
- `Crowd::solve` stopped a member whose finite wish had components near `f32::MAX`: the sideways nudge (`pref + pref.perp() * nudge`) overflowed to infinity, which `new_velocity_in` refuses with `Vec2::ZERO`. A nudge whose sum is not finite is no longer applied and the wish is solved as given; this also covers a `nudge` that is NaN, which used to stop every member that had a neighbour.
- `Crowd::solve` kicked each member's wish toward a side hashed from its `key`, so a crowd's kicks mostly cancelled and half of all head-on key pairs were kicked toward the same side of the line. Two agents walking straight at each other sidestepped unequally in 128 of 256 key pairs (the longer by up to 22%), and the circle swap at the defaults (`CrowdParams::new(3.0)`, 1 m of ring per agent) stalled agents for over two seconds: 33 of 100 and 606 of 1 000 in the bench, with 100 agents taking 61 s to get 90% through. Every member is now kicked toward the same side (see Changed): none stalls, 100 agents take 35 s and 1 000 take 266 s against 347 s, and the deepest overlap in the 1 000-agent swap falls from 0.144 to 0.017 of a radius. Over eight crowds per scene the bench's deepest overlap is at or below what it was in every case but the 100-agent corridor, whose rises from about 0.026 to about 0.037 of a radius (3 mm at 0.3 m); walls stay at exactly 0, and the bottleneck's flow through the gap is unchanged inside the spread between crowds.

### Changed

- **`CrowdParams` has a new field, `obstacle_horizon`** (default 0.5 s in `CrowdParams::new`), so a `CrowdParams { .. }` written out in full needs it (use `..CrowdParams::new(range)`); a struct-update expression and every call of `new` is unaffected. This is a breaking change to a public struct, so the next release is 0.3.0 by the crate's own rule, not 0.2.2. It is read only by `solve_around`; `solve` and every answer of a crowd without obstacles are bit-identical to before.
- `Crowd::solve` leaves a member whose position, velocity or radius is unusable out of everyone's neighbour list, so it takes none of the `max_neighbours` places; such a member with a finite position is solved to `Vec2::ZERO` rather than to a NaN.
- `CrowdParams::nudge`'s sign is now the side of the kick, the same for every member: a positive value kicks toward the right of the wish (clockwise in the plane the members are given in, the side ORCA's own tie-break sends an exactly head-on pair to), a negative one toward the left. `CrowdMember::key` no longer has any part in it. The default, `0.05`, is unchanged, so every solved velocity of a crowd that has neighbours changes, and so do the bench's hashes; they are still equal across member order and thread counts. A crowd crossing a crowd turns one way (counter-clockwise round a point for a positive `nudge`) where it used to turn whichever way its keys leaned; a two-way corridor only leans toward a lane per direction.
- A wall normal must now be a unit vector to `Vec2::is_normalized`'s tolerance, as the docs always said; a zero or scaled normal used to be a weaker or absent constraint and now holds the agent still.
- Every solved velocity of a step that has a constraint can change in its last bits, because the lines are stored differently (see Fixed), so a host that hashes its simulation state sees new hashes once; they remain equal across member order and thread count. Same-process paired timing against 0.2.1 (old, old, new, new on one frozen crowd, thread CPU time; the A/A floor was within ±0.7% at 1 000 agents and up to ±2.7% in the shortest and the longest runs): +0.0% to +4.4% per `Crowd::solve`, by scene and size: open +0.0 to +0.4%, circle swap +1.4 to +3.6%, corridor +1.2 to +4.3% (the larger at 100 agents), bottleneck +0.3 to +2.6%, rally +0.1 to +2.6%.
- A wall normal inside `Vec2::is_normalized`'s tolerance is divided by its length before use, so the wall is exactly `v · b <= approach` and its direction a unit vector; before, a normal of length `1 ± 1e-4` scaled the half-plane by `|b|²`. A normal that is not a unit vector to that tolerance is still refused with `Vec2::ZERO`.

## [0.2.1] - 2026-10-30

### Added

- `examples/bench.rs`: what one `Crowd::solve` step costs and what it gets right, in four scenes (open field, circle swap, two-way corridor with walls, bottleneck) at 100 to 10 000 agents — median and p99 step, agents per millisecond, neighbour candidates per agent, the 1k→10k scaling exponent, the deepest overlap and wall penetration, allocations inside the solve, and a state hash that must match across member order and the caller's parallel solve at 2 and the default thread count. An `arrive` section reports arrivals and stalls in the circle swap and a two-way corridor.

### Fixed

- `Crowd::solve` allocated whenever a warm crowd walked into ground it had not covered before (2 619 allocations in 480 steps of a 1 000-agent circle swap), and its per-step cost grew with the ground covered, because the neighbour grid kept a list for every cell it had ever seen. The grid is now a sorted `(cell, member)` index rebuilt every solve: its size is the crowd's, and a warm `Crowd` allocates nothing wherever the crowd walks. Neighbour lists, and so every solved velocity, are bit-identical to before.
- `Crowd::solve` panicked in a debug build (`attempt to add with overflow`) when a member's finite position was far enough out to saturate its grid cell, such as `x = 1e30`.
- The crate docs and `NOTICE` cited DOI `10.1109/TRO.2011.2120810` for "Reciprocal n-Body Collision Avoidance"; that DOI is "The Hybrid Reciprocal Velocity Obstacle". The ORCA paper is DOI `10.1007/978-3-642-19457-3_1`.
